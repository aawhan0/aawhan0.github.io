ch 01 · 3am, bhopal

i open the laptop
before i open the curtains.

It started as a party trick. A friend would complain that some tool half-worked, and before they finished the sentence I'd already be mentally rewriting it. Not to be helpful — just because the broken version sat in my head worse than the conversation did.

Somewhere around 2am in a hostel room in Bhopal it stopped being a trick. I'd be wide awake convinced I could rebuild it faster alone than we could together. I was usually wrong. I did it anyway. Still do.

ch 02 · thirty runs

i don't believe a good
number until i've tried
to break it.

Here's the slightly uncool part. Thirty runs, three scenarios, then I run them again because one result looked suspiciously good and I don't believe good luck. I have re-run a benchmark that was already in my favour purely to see whether it would hold.

It isn't discipline, honestly. It's that if something I built is quietly wrong, I need to be the one who finds it — not the user, and not some engineer six months from now. That's how I ended up watching a 10%-accuracy model say it was 90% sure. I'd rather know.

ch 03 · zero watchers

the best week of a build
is the one where nobody
posts about it.

Shipping something I'm genuinely proud of and having nobody to tell. Not sad, exactly. Just a specific kind of quiet. "Oh, you do AI stuff?" and the conversation moves on, and I'm back in a repo with zero watchers and zero stars, and I keep going anyway.

That hasn't changed now that the degree is done. If anything it's sharper — everyone around me has an offer letter and I'm the one still refreshing GitHub. I've done the imposter maths. I just refuse to let "is this good enough" be the question that decides it.

ch 04 · the honest ledger

i graduated in august.
here's what the degree
actually bought me.

The useful part: data structures, operating systems, DBMS, and three years of being wrong in public so cheaply that it stopped mattering. B.Tech CSE, 7.66 CGPA — and I would not have traded the semester where everything I built got ignored.

The useless part: nobody has ever hired me for a CGPA. Not one interview opened because of a grade. What actually got me through the door was a registry with a passing test suite, a retrieval benchmark with real numbers, and two merged PRs into code I did not write.

The new routine is worse for the social part and better for the work. No lectures to sit through, so the whole night is mine. I say no to a lot of things I would rather be doing. I picked that trade deliberately, and I would pick it again this month.

ch 05 · out loud

i publish the wrong
takes on purpose.

I read a paper, write it up badly, post it, and get politely corrected by someone much sharper. Which sounds like a bad system until you notice it is the fastest way I have found to discover I misunderstood something while it is still cheap to fix.
