ch 01 · 3am

i wake up with
tabs already open.

It started as a party trick. A friend would complain about some tool that half-worked, and before they finished the sentence I'd already be mentally rewriting it — not to be helpful, just because the broken version bothered me more than the conversation did.

Somewhere around 2am in a Bhopal hostel that stopped being a trick. I'd be lying awake convinced I could fix it faster alone than we could together, and usually I was wrong. But I'd do it anyway. Still do. That's the whole origin story — there isn't a better one.

ch 02 · nobody's watching

the loneliest
part is 4am.

Shipping something I'm genuinely proud of, and having nobody to tell. Not sad — just quiet in a way that's hard to explain to people who ask what I do. "Oh, you build AI stuff?" and then the conversation moves on, and I go back to a repo with zero watchers and zero stars and I keep going anyway.

I'm a final-year student surrounded by people already employed at companies I'd have killed for a year ago. I've done the imposter maths. I keep building through it, mostly by refusing to let the question "is this good enough" be the one that decides.

ch 03 · the thing i can't stop

i run the eval
again.

Here's something slightly uncool about me: I don't trust a thing until I've tried to break it on purpose. Thirty runs. Three scenarios. Re-run them because one result looked suspiciously good and I don't believe good luck.

It's not discipline, honestly. It's that if something I built turns out to be quietly wrong, I need to be the one who finds it — not the user, and not some engineer six months later. I caught a 10% accuracy model being 90% confident about its wrongness that way. I'd rather know. Always have.

ch 04 · the actual day

college in the morning,
the repo at night.

So this is the unsexy version. Eight-ish lecture, back by four, dinner with whoever's around, then the screen becomes mine until it's genuinely too late to be making sense. That stretch is most of why I finished a client site in three months and ~50 repos in three years.

The trade is real and I'm not going to dress it up. I say no to a lot of things I'd rather be doing. But I picked this deliberately, and I'd pick it again this month.

ch 05 · out loud

i post things
i get wrong, publicly.

I read a paper, write it up badly, post it, and get politely corrected by someone much smarter. Which sounds like a bad system until you realise it's the fastest way I've found to find out I misunderstood something while it was still cheap to fix.

The stuff that actually shipped — the model registry, the grounded retrieval thing, the incident tools — is all just this habit pointed at bigger problems. the list is on stage →

ch 06 · off the clock

boxing keeps
music honest.

When I'm not shipping, I'm on the heavy bag or at a midi keyboard. Boxing is the only place where overcommitting on vibes gets you hit, which makes it the honest counterweight to a job spent trusting language models. Amateur, still undefeated against the bag.

Music is the part nobody was ever going to ask me about at a conference. I write and release as aawhan — lo-fi confessionals for the 2am crowd, Joji-to-Yung-Lean territory. there's a room for it →
